About WL Predictor

Understanding the mathematical model and real-world factors behind railway waitlist confirmation.

Demystifying Waitlist Uncertainty

When booking Indian Railways tickets, passengers frequently receive a waiting list status (WL) rather than a confirmed berth. Travelers are often left guessing whether their ticket has a realistic chance of confirming before the train pulls away from the station.

WL Predictor is built as an open, educational platform that analyzes historical ticket movement velocity, seat inventory across coach classes, quota cancellation rules, and remaining days to deliver transparent, explainable confirmation estimates.

The 6 Scoring Pillars

Our algorithm follows a structured mathematical framework designed to be 100% explainable:

Pillar 1

Baseline Anchor (50%)

Every prediction starts with a balanced neutral base score of 50%, serving as the statistical median before individual ticket conditions are factored in.

Pillar 2

Current Position Impact

A lower waiting list number increases the score significantly (+25% for WL 1-5), whereas deep waitlists (>90) face heavy mathematical penalties (-32%).

Pillar 3

Time Remaining Window

More days remaining allow more passengers to cancel plans (+15% for >15 days). Conversely, tickets with ≤1 day left incur penalties (-18%) due to chart preparation.

Pillar 4

Daily Clearance Velocity

Computes the average daily drop from historical status tracking. Fast-moving waitlists (+16%) confirm much quicker than sluggish or stagnant queues.

Pillar 5

Class & Quota Quirks

Sleeper (+10%) and 3A (+5%) have many coaches and high turnover. Tatkal (-16%) and 1A (-14%) have low cancellations due to strict refund rules and limited berths.

Pillar 6

Historical Cancellations

Monitors route-specific daily cancellation rates from the database to reflect seasonal travel fluctuations and peak-period seat releases.

Confirmation Probability Categories

High (70%+ Chance)

Very high likelihood of securing RAC or a confirmed berth before train departure.

Medium (40% - 69%)

Moderate clearing potential. Usually progresses to RAC by final charting.

Low (Below 40%)

Low probability. Highly recommended to explore alternative travel dates or buses.

Important System Disclaimer

This is an estimate, not a guarantee. All confirmation probabilities generated by WL Predictor are calculated using statistical approximations and historical trends. Final seat allocations, RAC assignments, and chart releases are handled exclusively by official railway ticketing systems and cannot be guaranteed by any third-party tool.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does RAC mean?

RAC stands for Reservation Against Cancellation. An RAC passenger is guaranteed travel on the train and is allocated a sitting berth (shared side-lower berth) which may upgrade to a full sleeper berth if further cancellations occur.

When are railway reservation charts prepared?

The initial reservation chart is typically prepared 4 hours prior to the train's scheduled departure from originating station. A final chart is published approximately 30 minutes before departure.

Why do Tatkal waitlists confirm less frequently?

Confirmed Tatkal tickets grant zero refund upon cancellation. Because passengers forfeit the entire fare if they cancel, almost nobody cancels confirmed Tatkal tickets, leaving few vacancies for Tatkal waitlists.
